# Bias assessment

## Outcome measurement

FSW counts and density are mapping estimates assembled for HIV programming. They are vulnerable to undercoverage, variable field intensity, mobility, hidden-network visibility, and local classification practice. The analysis treats them as measured programmatic estimates, not true population counts.

## Exposure measurement

The primary exposure is a household's retrospective comparison with one year earlier. It is subjective, may reflect changing expectations, and is measured for a district urban stratum rather than the exact city population. Secondary deprivation measures capture different constructs and are used for triangulation, not interchangeability.

## Spatial alignment

City outcomes are linked to district urban strata. This can create exposure misclassification, especially for district proxies and metropolitan Karachi. Match grades and restricted analyses make this visible but cannot eliminate it.

## Temporal alignment

The paired survey years are close, not always identical: 2006–07 with PSLM 2006–07, 2011 mapping with PSLM 2010–11, and Punjab 2014 mapping with PSLM 2014–15. Round 5 mapping/IBBS timing spans 2015–17 and is context-only.

## Confounding and ecological inference

Urbanization, migration, policing, transport corridors, program maturity, venue visibility, and local social organization may affect both measured distress and mapped sex-work structure. With 15 primary observations, covariate adjustment is sharply limited. Associations cannot be translated into individual-level causal claims.

## Selection and multiplicity

Cities were selected by surveillance-program priorities rather than probability sampling of all Pakistani cities. One primary test is prespecified; secondary tests are labeled and structural replication p-values receive FDR correction.
