# Literature-review protocol

## Review question

What does existing research say about the relationship between economic conditions and the size, entry, exit, persistence, mobility, visibility, and organisation of female sex work, and how should that evidence be used to interpret the complete available Pakistan surveillance series?

## Scope

The review prioritised:

1. Pakistan studies and official reports that use, describe, or cite the same surveillance and mapping system as Paper 1.
2. Zimbabwe studies because they directly examine economic collapse, recovery, demand, migration, and reconfiguration of sex work and provide the closest identified comparison.
3. Comparable evidence from other settings on economic shocks, recession, transactional sex, entry, exit, re-entry, persistence, hidden/informal work, and population-size measurement.
4. Systematic reviews and structural frameworks needed to keep economic interpretation from becoming an overly simple poverty-only story.

Searches were conducted on 2026-10-03. Sources already acquired for the reproducible Pakistan analysis were also screened.

## Search approach

This was a structured, iterative search of PubMed/PMC, publisher sites, DOI records, official Pakistani sources, institutional repositories, and general scholarly web search. Search concepts were combined around:

- Pakistan + female sex workers + mapping / IBBS / population size / structure / economic / poverty;
- Zimbabwe + sex work + economic collapse / recovery / migration / demand / reconfiguration;
- sex work + recession / economic decline / income shock / drought / livelihood;
- sex work + entry / leaving / exit / re-entry / persistence / occupational duration;
- transactional sex + economic shock + systematic review;
- hidden populations + mapping + population-size estimation.

The exact search themes and representative query strings are in `search_log.csv`.

## Inclusion rules

A source was included when it contributed at least one of the following:

- direct evidence from the Pakistani FSW surveillance system used by Paper 1;
- empirical evidence linking economic conditions or shocks to sex-work or transactional-sex supply, demand, organisation, or risk;
- evidence about entry, exit, re-entry, or barriers that informs the plateau interpretation;
- evidence about changing visibility, location, intermediation, or mobility;
- methodological evidence about the interpretation of mapped hidden-population counts;
- a high-value review or framework that synthesises structural mechanisms.

## Exclusion rules

The review excluded news stories, unsourced advocacy claims, duplicate records, papers solely about HIV treatment without an economic/structural or measurement contribution, studies only of male or transgender sex workers unless directly needed for surveillance context, and sources for which the relevant claim could not be verified.

## Appraisal

Each record was coded for design, sample/scope, economic driver, sex-work outcome, direction relative to the Paper 1 interpretation, evidence grade, directness, and major limitations. Stronger causal language is reserved for quasi-experimental or longitudinal evidence. Cross-sectional, qualitative, and ecological results are described as associations, accounts, or patterns.

## Copyright and abstract handling

Full publisher abstracts are not stored. The repository contains original abstract-style summaries plus links to the official abstract or full text. Any future direct quotation must be checked against the source and kept within publisher and copyright requirements.

## Limits of this review

- It is comprehensive for the specific mechanisms relevant to Paper 1, but it is not a PRISMA-registered systematic review.
- No subscription-only database export (for example, Embase or Scopus) was available for exhaustive deduplication and citation chasing.
- Grey literature is difficult to index, especially for Pakistan; the official reports already used in the analysis are therefore treated as essential primary sources.
- “Transactional sex” is not identical to formally identified sex work. Those studies support an economic coping mechanism, not a one-to-one population comparison.
- HIV or STI outcomes are not population-size outcomes. They are retained only where the economic or structural pathway is informative.
- Cross-country transfer is conceptual. Legal regimes, stigma, female labour markets, client demand, mapping methods, and social organisation differ.

## Update rule

Before manuscript submission, rerun the dated searches, check for a newer Pakistan IBBS/mapping round, verify every DOI and URL, and add any newly eligible studies without deleting this frozen 2026-10-03 search record.
