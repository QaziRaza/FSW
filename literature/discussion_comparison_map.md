## The Zimbabwe comparison in one paragraph

Elmes et al. (ZW01) is the closest result-level comparison: during economic collapse, Zimbabwe’s sex trade did not simply expand or contract as one visible market. Formal commercial demand weakened, while poverty-driven sexual exchange became more dispersed, informal, private, and sometimes commodity based. Steen et al. (ZW02) supplies the complementary demand-side evidence: layoffs and loss of migrant wages reduced client demand and client volumes, which later recovered. Together, the studies predict exactly why a Pakistan analysis can find expanding mapped populations and home/intermediary reorganisation without a clean positive density relationship. They also explain why recovery could produce a plateau rather than a collapse in the existing population.
